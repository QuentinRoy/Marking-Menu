PROTOTYPE — measure-and-solve label placement (#267)

Question: is a measure-then-grow-the-radius loop an acceptable way to place label plates around the wedge ring, and what does it actually cost and guarantee? This page renders real DOM plates (real fonts, real CSS box model, the content-hug + clamp rule from #276), measures them with getBoundingClientRect, and runs two solvers side by side: the design canvas's 2px linear scan, and a closed-form direct solve. Compare their radius, iteration count and timing; stress-test with more/longer items; and check determinism across repeated measurement and simulated browser zoom.

Neither solver above checks two things a plate must also respect: not covering a different item's connector, and not sitting on top of the ring itself. A radius that clears every pair of plates can still let a wide plate sit across a neighbor's connector line, or dip inside the ring's own selection area. The "Long labels" and "Manual angles" presets show both happening; the third solver, "connector-safe", raises the floor to clear the ring under every item, then finds the exact radius windows where a connector would be covered and pushes past them, still without scanning. See the Solve panel.

Four separate clearances feed the solve, and they are easy to mix up because two of them share a word: the plate-plate gaps set how far two different plates must stay from each other (horizontal and vertical are separate because the plates are wider than they are tall, so a pair stacked vertically needs much less slack than a pair side by side); the plate-ring clearance sets how far every plate stays from the ring, and did not exist as its own knob until now: the earlier version placed plates with zero clearance, right up against the ring's edge. Try "Plate–plate horizontal gap" alone on the default preset: it does nothing up to its slider maximum, because for this menu's label shapes the vertical gap (or the ring clearance) is always the tighter constraint first.
